Neil Denby

Technical Experience, Family Commitment and Independent Representation

Neil Denby, Independent candidate for Hauraki-Waikato, brings approximately 50 years of technical experience, a commitment to family care and firsthand knowledge of the challenges facing people in smaller towns and rural communities. His working life was built in aircraft electronics, technical instruction, maintenance management and electronic security. His involvement in politics came after retirement, following a career centred on practical work and responsibility.

Neil joined the Royal New Zealand Air Force in 1968, training as an Aircraft Radio Technician before the trade broadened into avionics. His service included work with 40 Squadron, instruction at Wigram from 1983 to 1986, and management of helicopter maintenance schedules at Hobsonville. He retired from the RNZAF in 1991 and continued his technical career in civilian roles, including technical training and electronic security.

His qualifications reflect the breadth of that experience. He obtained Radio Serviceman registration through the Electricians’ Registration Board of New Zealand on 23 February 1979. In 1980, he completed a three-week High Reliability Hand Soldering course at the Royal Australian Air Force School of Radio, with his certificate dated 9 May 1980. In February 2013, he was awarded the National Certificate in Electronic Security (Systems Installation), Level 3, through The Skills Organisation. His earlier certificates record his full name as Neil McLean Denby.

Across these roles, Neil worked in fields where accuracy, dependable systems and clear instructions mattered. Aircraft maintenance and technical training required careful attention to detail, practical judgment and responsibility for work that others relied upon.

Neil retired in April 2016, after approximately half a century in a technical career. Retirement brought a significant family responsibility. His mother-in-law had just turned 90 and had made her wishes clear: she did not want to move into what she called an “old people’s home”. She wanted her family to help care for her.

Neil and his wife undertook that care. With his wife’s brother living next door, it became a shared family commitment. His mother-in-law lived with them until she passed away at 95. During those years, she gained many friends and was well respected by those who knew her.

For Neil, this experience reinforced the importance of family support and of respecting older people’s wishes. He believes that, wherever circumstances allow, families should have the opportunity to help their older relatives remain within a familiar and caring environment. His understanding of care comes from the everyday responsibilities of providing it over several years.

After retiring and moving to Matamata, Neil became active in politics. In the 2020 general election campaign, he served as Campaign Manager for New Conservative’s Waikato candidate. This gave him direct experience of organising a local campaign and engaging with the electoral process.

During the 2023 general election, Neil participated in the Election Integrity Project, which was involved in scrutineering election voting throughout the country. He describes that work as very challenging. His involvement added another dimension to his experience of elections, alongside his earlier campaign-management role.

Now standing as an Independent, Neil brings that experience together with the perspective developed through his technical career, family responsibilities and life in Matamata.

Conversations with local residents have also highlighted the practical effect of rising household costs. Speaking with people in Waharoa, only a short distance from Matamata, Neil heard that fuel costs were forcing some residents to limit their trips into town.

These accounts brought home how even a relatively short journey can become a financial consideration. For people living farther from town, the challenge can be greater still. Shopping, appointments, family commitments and other essential activities may require careful planning, combining several purposes into one trip and sharing transport where possible.

Neil sees these conversations as a reminder that transport costs affect more than the household fuel bill. They influence people’s ability to reach services, maintain social connections and participate in community life. The experiences of residents in places such as Waharoa deserve to be heard when decisions affecting rural and small-town communities are made.

Neil’s background combines technical knowledge with practical service: maintaining aircraft systems, teaching technical skills, managing maintenance responsibilities, caring for an elderly family member and participating in election work. These experiences inform the perspective he brings to public life.

As an Independent candidate for Hauraki-Waikato, Neil offers voters a representative whose political involvement follows decades of work and family responsibility. His biography is grounded in practical experience, continued learning and a willingness to take on demanding tasks—whether in a technical workplace, within his family or in the community.
